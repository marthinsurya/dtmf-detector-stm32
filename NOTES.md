# GLOSSARY
DTMF -  Dual-Tone Multi-Frequency
Q15 - fixed-point format: int16 where the value is x / 32768, range [-1, 1)
Goertzel - recursion that gives the energy of one frequency bin in N samples (cheaper than a full FFT for 8 tones)

# Step 2 Notes (C, Q15)
Lessons from porting the float Goertzel to Q15. Result so far: Q15 detector 16/16/15/6 correct at SNR 20/0/-10/-20 (16 keys each), float reference 16/16/16/7.

1. **Overflow.** First attempt kept the state in int16 and `q15_mul(int16)` silently truncated it. The state reaches about ±2e6 (22 bits), so it needs int32; the energy needs int64.
2. **Truncate vs round.** `(int16_t)x` truncates toward zero and gives a small bias on every conversion. `float_to_q15` rounds with `roundf` instead.
3. **-1 * -1 saturates.** In Q15, -32768 * -32768 would be +1.0, which does not fit (max 32767/32768). The multiply must saturate.
4. **Input clipping.** The test signal peaks at about 2.2, outside the Q15 range [-1, 1). `INPUT_SCALE` 0.25 keeps it in range and leaves headroom in the state.
5. **Scaling.** The 0.25 scale multiplies all energies by 1/16, so argmax and the key decision are unchanged. Only compare absolute energies to float/Python after multiplying by 16.
6. **Silent int64 -> float.** Storing the int64 energy in a `float` array compiled without warnings and rounds to 24 bits. Keep the energy as int64 end to end.
7. **Recompute inside the loop.** `coeff * s1` depends on the current `s1`, so it must be recomputed every iteration, not once before the loop. Cast to int64 before multiplying, otherwise int16 * int32 is computed in int32.
8. **Loop nesting.** A loop variable reused/shadowed in nested loops gave wrong results without any error.
9. **Coefficients.** The 8 coefficients are Q15 constants generated by `tools/gen_coeffs.c`, so no `cosf` is needed in the detector (and none on the MCU).

Open: the 15/6 vs 16/7 gap is probably Q15 quantization at low SNR on near-tie signals. 16 keys per SNR is too few to tell, so step 2d repeats with 200 trials per SNR and tunes `INPUT_SCALE`.
