# GLOSSARY
DTMF -  Dual-Tone Multi-Frequency
Q15 - fixed-point format: int16 where the value is x / 32768, range [-1, 1)
Goertzel - recursion that gives the energy of one frequency bin in N samples (cheaper than a full FFT for 8 tones)

# Step 2 Notes (C, Q15)
Fixed-point pitfalls met when porting the float Goertzel to Q15. Result so far: Q15 detector 16/16/15/6 correct at SNR 20/0/-10/-20 (16 keys each), float reference 16/16/16/7.

1. **Overflow.** The Goertzel state grows to about ±2e6 (22 bits), far outside int16, so it is kept in int32 and the energy in int64. Products are cast to int64 before multiplying; otherwise int16 * int32 is evaluated in int32 and can overflow.
2. **Rounding.** `(int16_t)x` truncates toward zero and adds a small bias on every conversion. `float_to_q15` rounds with `roundf` instead.
3. **Saturation.** In Q15, -32768 * -32768 would be +1.0, which does not fit (max 32767/32768). The multiply must saturate.
4. **Input clipping.** The test signal peaks at about 2.2, outside the Q15 range [-1, 1). `INPUT_SCALE` 0.25 keeps it in range and leaves headroom in the state.
5. **Scaling.** The 0.25 scale multiplies all energies by 1/16, so the argmax and the key decision are unchanged. Absolute energies are only comparable to float/Python after multiplying by 16.
6. **Coefficients.** The 8 coefficients cos(2*pi*f/FS) are Q15 constants generated by `tools/gen_coeffs.c`, so no `cosf` is needed in the detector (or on the MCU).

Open: the 15/6 vs 16/7 gap is probably Q15 quantization at low SNR on near-tie signals. 16 keys per SNR is too few to tell, so step 2d repeats with 200 trials per SNR and tunes `INPUT_SCALE`.
